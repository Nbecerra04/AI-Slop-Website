Constitution Gardens

Fifty quiet acres on the National Mall. Walk under willows, circle the lake, and cross to an island that honors the 56 signers of the Declaration of Independence.

A park made to rest in

Constitution Gardens sits between the Lincoln Memorial and the Washington Monument, just off Constitution Avenue. It opened in 1976 for the nation's bicentennial and was designed as a green pause in the middle of the Mall's monuments.

A wide loop path circles a lake of roughly six acres. Ducks, geese, and turtles share the water, and benches face the shore in every direction. It is one of the calmer places to eat lunch downtown.

The island of signatures

A footbridge leads to a small island in the lake. There, 56 granite blocks carry the names and signatures of the men who signed the Declaration of Independence in 1776. The memorial was dedicated in 1984. Read them slowly; the handwriting is the point.

  • John Hancock
  • Benjamin Franklin
  • Thomas Jefferson
  • John Adams
  • Charles Carroll
  • Button Gwinnett

What to do here

Walk the loop

The lake path is flat and easy, about a mile with the detours. Good for strollers and wheelchairs.

Feed your eyes, not the ducks

Wildlife is wild. Watch from the shore and leave bread at home.

Picnic under a willow

Grab a bench or spread a blanket on the lawn. Pack out what you bring.

Visit at golden hour

Late light on the water makes this the best view of the Washington Monument's reflection.

Pair it with a memorial

The Vietnam Veterans Memorial and the Lincoln Memorial are a few minutes' walk west.

Come in spring

Cherry blossom season fills the Mall, and the gardens stay far less crowded than the Tidal Basin.

Plan your visit

Location: Along Constitution Avenue NW, between 17th and 23rd Streets NW, Washington, DC.

Getting there: The closest Metrorail stops are Foggy Bottom-GWU (Blue, Orange, Silver) and Smithsonian, each about a 15 to 20 minute walk. Parking on the Mall is very limited, so transit, biking, or the DC Circulator are easier.
